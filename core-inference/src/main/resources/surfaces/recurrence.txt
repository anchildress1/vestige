## Surface: Recurrence

A RETRIEVED HISTORY chunk with a `pattern_id` is a deterministic *candidate*: prior entries that share
this entry's time slot. Your job is to decide whether that candidate is a real recurrence or a coincidence.

What to capture:

- Whether this entry repeats the behavior or state shift the candidate's prior entries describe.
- Whether the recurrence is exact (same activity + same context) or partial (same activity, different context).

The viability test (the whole point):

- A shared time slot is NOT recurrence. "I happened to log at this hour again" is not a pattern.
- Link only when the same behavior or state genuinely recurs — e.g. the energy crash returns after each
  meeting, not merely that the entries land at the same clock time.
- When the candidate is only a logging coincidence, omit `recurrence_link`. The Skeptical lens additionally
  raises an `unsupported-recurrence` flag for a candidate it judges an artifact.

What goes in the schema:

- `recurrence_link`: copy the candidate's `pattern_id` verbatim when it genuinely recurs; omit otherwise.
- `recurrence_kind`: `exact` or `partial`, only alongside a `recurrence_link` line.
- Do not encode the pattern id into `tags`. The `recurrence_link` line is the only place pattern ids belong.
- Ignore list ordinals, bullets, and chunk numbers. Only copy the identifier that appears after `pattern_id=`.

Constraints:

- Do not invent a `pattern_id`. Use only IDs present in the retrieved-history block.
- Retrieved chunks without a `pattern_id` are context-only — they inform the reading but cannot populate `recurrence_link`.
- Do not infer recurrence from semantic similarity alone. Only a chunk's `pattern_id` can be linked.
- No retrieved-history block means no `recurrence_link`. Normal for early entries.
